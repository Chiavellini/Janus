## The central observation

Calibration of entry price against realized outcome, at three price anchors:

| Bucket | first trade | @1h | @6h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.05 | +7.03pp | +3.12pp | **−0.14pp** |
| 0.10–0.20 | +11.12pp | +7.45pp | +2.32pp |
| 0.35–0.50 | −4.18pp | −8.28pp | −6.53pp |
| 0.65–0.80 | −17.38pp | −21.18pp | **−14.19pp** |
| 0.90–0.95 | −20.89pp | −15.76pp | −10.84pp |
| 0.95–1.01 | **−46.09pp** | −12.06pp | **−0.60pp** |

Two distinct things are happening and they must not be conflated:

1. **The extremes are a price-discovery artifact.** The 0.95+ bucket goes −46pp at the first print
   to −0.60pp by 6h; 0.00–0.05 goes +7.03pp to −0.14pp. Early prints are not real prices — with a
   median first trade **7,054s (~2h) after open** and only **5.8% of markets trading within 5
   minutes**, the first print is typically a lone taker hitting a stale resting quote. **Do not
   build anything on `first_trade_price`.**
2. **A residual persists in the middle after 6h of discovery** — 0.65–0.80 still sits at −14.19pp
   (buying NO would return +12.19¢/contract at real Kalshi fees). That is the only part worth a
   hypothesis.

**A censoring explanation was tested and REJECTED.** These markets close early when the word is
said, so I expected "has a 6h price" to select against YES outcomes and manufacture the residual.
It does not: markets with a 6h price realize YES **0.4376** vs **0.4208** without — a 1.7pp
difference in the *wrong* direction. The markets missing a 6h price are missing for **illiquidity**
(median 5 trades, 72h lifetime) not early resolution. The residual survives this check.

## H1 — Favourite overpricing in mention markets *(primary)*

**Claim.** Mention contracts priced 0.65–0.95 after meaningful price discovery are systematically
overpriced; selling them (buying NO) is +EV net of real fees.

**Why it might be real.** "Will they say X" is a lottery-ticket framing that attracts yes-buyers,
and Kalshi mention markets are thinly two-sided (measured live: bid 0.04 / ask 0.78). Kilos found
the same direction on Equity weekly ladders (far-OTM implied 0.239 vs 0.138 realized).

**Why it might not.** The 6h price may not be *transactable* — a mid with no depth is not an entry.
This is the single most likely killer and it is **not yet measured**.

### H1 STATUS: **DOWNGRADED — do not spend a look.** Robustness battery run 2026-07-25.

Band 0.35–0.95 at the discovered 6h price, n=4,804 across 119 series, buying NO:

| Execution assumption | EV/contract | day-block CI | |
|---|---|---|---|
| at the traded price (optimistic) | **+5.79¢** | [+3.94, +7.62] | excl-0 |
| + 3.5¢ half-spread (median book) | **+2.18¢** | [+0.33, +4.01] | barely excl-0 |
| **+ 7¢ full spread (stress)** | **−1.36¢** | [−3.21, +0.47] | **includes 0** |

It fails on four independent axes:

1. **Execution.** Buying NO requires crossing. At the measured median 7¢ spread the edge is gone.
2. **Concentration.** Top-5 of 119 series = **81.3% of all PnL**; only 64/119 series are positive.
3. **Leave-one-series-out.** Dropping any single top series collapses it to ~+1.8–2.0¢ with the CI
   **including zero in 5 of 8** cases. No single series may be removed without breaking it.
4. **Fold stability: FAIL.** 53.9% of 89 day-folds positive (bar 60%), worst fold −52¢,
   `fold_robust: False`.

**Verdict: not worth a snooping look.** The apparent +8.71¢ was a mid-price artifact concentrated
in a handful of thin series. Spending a one-shot on this would have burned a look and tightened the
BH bar on everything else for a hypothesis that cannot survive its own execution costs.

*This is the battery working as intended: the loop generated the hypothesis and then killed it,
before any holdout data was touched.*

**H3 is downgraded with it, for the same reason and with a direct demonstration:**
`KXPSAKIMENTION` showed a −30.19pp gap at n=70 and **+0.80pp at n=224** once the corpus grew.
Per-series gaps at small n are noise.

## H2 — Transactability *(BLOCKING — RUN 2026-07-25, result below)*

**Claim.** The price is a real, fillable level with depth on the side we would need.

**Result: PARTIAL PASS — spread is not the problem, depth is.** Live orderbook probe of 25 open
mention markets inside the H1 band [0.65, 0.95] (294 such markets open concurrently), label-free,
no outcomes, no snooping look:

| | |
|---|---|
| YES spread in-band | **median 7¢** (p25 6¢, p75 13¢) |
| Books with NO-side depth | **25/25**, 9–10 levels each |
| NO-side notional at the touch | **median $4**, p90 $46, max $58 |

**Correction to an earlier claim in this project.** I previously characterised Kalshi mention
spreads as "~74¢" from a live catalog sample (`bid 0.04 / ask 0.78`). That figure came from the
**KXMAMDANIMENTION** series specifically and is **not representative** — the in-band median is 7¢.
The "74¢ spread kills it" concern was wrong.

**What this does to H1.** Crossing a 7¢ spread costs ~3.5¢ of half-spread against a claimed −14pp
gap, so the edge is not erased by spread. It *is* capped by depth: **~$4 at the touch** means
per-market size is tiny. That makes this a many-small-positions strategy — 294 concurrent in-band
markets × ~10 book levels — which is consistent with the "high frequency, small profits" framing,
but it means **capacity, not edge, will be the binding constraint**, and any EV claim must be
computed on a depth-walked fill, never at the touch price.

**Still required before H1 is testable:** the same measurement at the *actual* entry moment
(open+6h) on forward data, since this probe sampled whatever moment the markets happened to be in.

## H3 — Series-level heterogeneity

Per-series gaps at first trade span **−37pp to +3.7pp**:

| Series | n | price | realized | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KXLASTWORDMENTION | 73 | 0.494 | 0.123 | −37.1pp |
| KXPSAKIMENTION | 70 | 0.473 | 0.171 | −30.2pp |
| KXLOVEISLMENTION | 497 | 0.549 | 0.300 | −24.9pp |
| KXTRUMPMENTION | 958 | 0.502 | 0.499 | **−0.4pp** |
| KXWCMENTION | 2335 | 0.464 | 0.475 | +1.1pp |

**Do not read this as "some series are exploitable."** It is measured at `first_trade_price`, which
§1 already establishes is not a real price, and the well-calibrated series (Trump, World Cup) are
exactly the liquid ones. The likely reading is that the gap measures **illiquidity, not mispricing**
— thin series have noisier first prints. Test only after H2, and only at a discovered price.

## H4 — Cross-venue mention pairs *(RUN 2026-07-25 — REFUTED)*

Matched Kalshi↔PM mention markets on (speaker, term), label-free, live quotes: **134 matched pairs,
52 with both sides two-sided.** As the spread filter tightens, the cross-venue divergence collapses:

| Both spreads ≤ | n | Kalshi mid − PM mid | \|d\| > 5pp |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100¢ | 52 | −2.46pp | 24/52 |
| 20¢ | 34 | +4.24pp | 6/34 |
| 10¢ | 30 | +1.27pp (median 0.00) | 3/30 |
| **5¢** | **28** | **+0.52pp (median 0.00)** | **1/28** |

**When both venues genuinely quote a mention market, they agree to within half a point.** The
Warsh FOMC markets — 1¢ spreads on both venues — agree to 0–3pp across the board. The apparent
large divergences are all wide-spread artifacts, and the survivors at ≤10¢ are MrBeast markets that
are plausibly *different questions* ("his next YouTube video" vs "his next **gaming** YouTube
video" both exist on PM). **A rules mismatch, not an arbitrage.**

**Correction to my own framing.** I had characterised this as "PM is tightly quoted, Kalshi is
not" and hoped PM's price would be an independent fair value against which Kalshi looked rich. In
the matched sample Kalshi's median spread is **1¢ — as tight as PM's**. Kalshi mention books are
not systematically wide; the earlier impression came from unrepresentative series.

**This is now the third independent confirmation of the same law in this project:** BTC 15m
windows, general event markets, and now mentions — *where the two venues genuinely list the same
market, they agree on price, and every apparent arbitrage is a spread artifact or a rules mismatch.*

**IMPORTANT — this does NOT validate H1, and it does not refute it either.** H1 is a single-venue
calibration claim (Kalshi prices vs realized outcomes), not a cross-venue claim. If Kalshi's
0.65–0.95 band really is ~10pp rich against realized frequency, and PM agrees with Kalshi to within
0.5pp, then **both venues carry the same bias**. Cross-venue agreement is evidence about
*efficiency between venues*, not about *calibration against reality*. Two markets can be wrong
together, and a shared framing effect ("will they say X" attracts yes-buyers on both venues) is
exactly the kind of thing that would produce that.
